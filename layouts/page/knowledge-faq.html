Compliance & Legal

Our KYC/AML process integrates with dedicated compliance infrastructure:

  1. Identity intake and document collection (operator-facing KYB wizard + dossier)
  2. Sanctions screening against OFAC lists (verdict surfaced for operator review)
  3. Risk-based enhanced due diligence for higher-risk verticals and jurisdictions
  4. Operator-facing review screens for transaction monitoring verdicts

Verification is submitted through the platform's compliance surfaces; screening results are typically available within 24-48 hours. Once verified, the client's status is valid across the operator's platform.

CCASH provides operator-facing compliance surfaces for money-services operators:

  • BSA/AML compliance surfaces: Operator-facing KYC/KYB intake and review screens, sanctions screening verdict display, transaction review, and SAR/STR drafting and filing
  • Regulatory alignment: Surfaces aligned with FinCEN and applicable US requirements for MSBs, VASPs, and PSPs
  • Multi-party control: Four-eyes principle for critical operations, tamper-evident audit trails, and role-based access controls

Our compliance surfaces are designed to be adapted — operators customize controls and workflows based on their specific regulatory obligations and risk profiles.

The CCASH platform enforces compliance controls at the application layer:

  • Verified participant identity required before any money movement
  • Risk-tiered transaction limits with configurable thresholds
  • Four-eyes approval gates for high-value or high-risk transfers
  • Client-type restrictions based on operator configuration

Transfers are only executed after passing all configured compliance gates.

Architecture Questions

The CCASH platform enforces a 4-layer cryptographic authority hierarchy — a chain of trust from root to leaf:

  1. Master Authority: Root authority, controlled by threshold signing. The sovereign key.
  2. Reseller Authority: Delegated from Master. Can issue License authority to platform installations.
  3. License Authority: Per-installation authority. Issues credentials to verified users.
  4. Share Authority: Per-domain access tokens - up to 10,000 per License. The leaf of the tree.

Every operation on the platform traces its authority back through this hierarchy. No authority, no access. No exceptions.

A Grain is the fundamental isolation unit in Sandstorm/Melusina OS. Each grain is a sandboxed application instance with its own append-only journal store, capability set, and lifecycle. Think of it as an indestructible, self-contained process that remembers everything.

The CCASH platform uses two grain types:

  • Station Grains (orchestrators): long-lived components that manage multiple workflows and participants
  • Instance Grains (workers): one per entity, one per workflow, isolated and sovereign

Grains communicate via Cap'n Proto RPC through the Powerbox. No HTTP bridge. No shared databases. Pure capability security.

Cap'n Proto is a zero-copy serialization protocol - data is read directly from the wire format with no encoding or decoding step. We use it for inter-grain RPC communication on FD3, enabling native Sandstorm integration without an HTTP bridge.

Our Cap'n Proto schemas define the capability interfaces for core platform operations, compliance verification, and governance actions.

The Powerbox is the inter-grain capability sharing mechanism. When one grain needs to grant another access, it doesn't share a password or API key - it offers a capability through the Powerbox.

The flow works like this:

  1. A grain publishes a Powerbox offer (e.g., a specific API capability)
  2. Another grain requests the capability via Powerbox claim
  3. Claim token is converted to a persistent sturdyRef
  4. The sturdyRef survives restarts and enables cross-session, cross-grain communication

Capabilities are fine-grained and revocable. Each grain receives only the capabilities it needs for its specific role.
